Smart Cart

A shopping cart that follows you through the store and bills your items automatically. Scan the QR code on the cart, tap "This is me" on your phone, and walk. The cart keeps 40 cm behind you, steers around obstacles, and adds each tagged product to your bill as you drop it in.

Everything runs on the cart with no GPU and no cloud vision: a Raspberry Pi 5 does the seeing and deciding, and three ESP32 boards handle motors, sensors and billing.

Raspberry Pi 5, ESP32, YOLOv8n, ByteTrack, Orbbec Astra Pro RGB-D, UHF RFID, Firebase, Flask, Tailscale

  1. SeeYOLOv8n in NCNN format finds people at 15–17 fps on the Pi's CPU. Depth from the RGB-D camera gives real distance in meters, and a colour-and-height check re-finds the right person after they're blocked.
  2. DecideA four-state controller (idle, armed, following, searching) sets speed and steering, holds still while you browse a shelf, and plans around obstacles with a vector field histogram.
  3. Move safelyAn ESP32 runs the motors with encoder and gyro feedback. A second reads five ultrasonic sensors, which can stop the cart whatever the camera says.
  4. BillA third ESP32 reads UHF RFID tags in the basket, looks each product up in Firebase, and never bills the same tag twice.
  5. ControlThe shopper's panel opens from a QR code on any phone over public HTTPS, protected by a token. A private admin dashboard shows the camera, live charts and diagnostics.

How it got here

  1. Bluetooth signal strengthThree ESP32s measured a beacon's signal to find the user's direction. Body blocking and reflections made the readings swing from -60 to -100 dBm, too noisy to steer by.
  2. Cart-XA camera tracker on a mecanum-wheel base with a Flutter app over BLE. It worked indoors, but had no real distance, drifted onto other people, and had no obstacle protection.

ABAC Flood Watch

September 2026

Why I made it. In late September 2026, days of heavy rain flooded the roads around ABAC's Suvarnabhumi campus. Many of us live in condos nearby, and the only way to know whether your soi was passable was to scroll through group chats or walk out and look. I wanted one page where any student could see the water level outside every condo at a glance, and add their own in a few seconds.

What it does. Students add their condo and pick what they see outside, from "Dry" to "Entrance cut off", with an optional short note. Every phone viewing the page updates in real time. Reports older than six hours are marked as old, and Thai emergency numbers are one tap away at the bottom.

How it's built. A single HTML file backed by Firebase Firestore, with anonymous sign-in so nobody needs an account, and security rules that check every field and block deleting other people's reports.

JavaScript, Firebase Firestore, Firebase Auth

Coffee bean quality classification

CE4715 Artificial Intelligence, 2026

I'm a coffee enjoyer, so this AI course project was close to home. We built two models that judge coffee in different ways and put them in one desktop app with two tabs.

Quality from taste scores. Trained on 1,517 real coffee samples, merged from a 2014–2016 GitHub dataset and a 2022–2023 Kaggle dataset. It predicts a Low, Medium or High quality tier from tasting scores. A search across four model types raised accuracy from an 87% baseline to 95% with logistic regression, and the app also suggests similar coffees.

Roast level from a photo. A MobileNetV2 model fine-tuned in PyTorch on 1,600 bean photos sorts beans into green, light, medium or dark roast, live from a webcam. The first version scored 99.5% on the test set but misread real phone photos of packed beans. Adding augmentation, partial fine-tuning and synthetic "packed pile" images fixed that, at 98.5% test accuracy that holds up on real photos.

Python, scikit-learn, PyTorch, MobileNetV2, OpenCV, Tkinter

Team project with Swam Yee Htet, Khant Min Lu, Sit Mue Naing and Chit Hnin Phyu.
